The legal half of security engineering: the rules that decide whether a technically sound system is
also a lawful one. Taught by a practising lawyer, examined orally on case studies.

## 📄 What's here

*   **[`study_summary.pdf`](./study_summary.pdf)** — condensed course notes covering the legal
    frameworks and the principles of data protection.

## 📚 Topics covered

**Legal foundations** — sources of law and their hierarchy, effectiveness of law in time and space,
interpretation, natural and legal persons, obligations and liability. The groundwork that makes the rest
readable.

**Privacy regulation** — the Italian framework, the contrasting U.S. model, and **GDPR
(Regulation EU 679/2016)** in depth: data subject rights, controller duties, lawful bases, and
compliance in practice. **UNI 11697:2017** on professional profiles in data protection. The **DPO**
role — appointment, independence, responsibilities. **Privacy by design and by default**. Information
security and risk management as legal obligations, not just engineering choices. **DPIA** — when it is
required and how it is conducted.

**Cybercrime and data protection** — unauthorised access to information systems, unlawful possession and
distribution of access codes, violation of electronic correspondence, damage to data and programs,
computer fraud and phishing, digital investigations and computer forensics.

## 🎯 Skills demonstrated

Reading a regulation and turning it into a design constraint — and the reverse, explaining a technical
architecture to someone who evaluates it in legal terms. The complement to
[Computer Security](../computer_security/): that course covers the ISO 27000 risk management machinery,
this one covers the obligations that make using it mandatory.
